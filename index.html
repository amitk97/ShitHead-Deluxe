    Guide & Strategy

    Comprehensive rules, card powers, tips and hotkeys

    No matching results found.

    Welcome to ShitHead! The objective is to shed all your cards across three sequential phases. The last remaining player holding cards is the Shithead.

    0. Swap Phase: Before the first card is played, everyone can swap cards between their Hand and their Face-Up Cards. Tap READY when you're happy (see Swap Phase below).

    1. Hand Phase: You must play cards from your Hand first. Whenever your Hand drops below 3 cards and the Deck has cards remaining, you automatically refill.

    2. Face-Up Table Phase: When your Hand and the Deck are completely empty, you play directly from your 3 Face-Up Cards.

    3. Blind Face-Down Phase: Once your Face-Up Cards are cleared, flip your 3 Face-Down Cards one by one. If you flip an illegal move, you must pick up the entire Pile!

    Equal or Higher: You must play a card that is equal to or higher than the current top card of the Pile (e.g. King on Queen), depending on what you are playing on.

    Automatic Pickup: If it is your turn and you hold no legal moves, the game automatically gives you the Pile without hassle.

    Every match starts with the Swap Phase. You're dealt 3 Face-Down Cards, 3 Face-Up Cards on top of them and 3 cards in your Hand. Before anyone plays, you can swap cards between your Hand and your Face-Up Cards as many times as you like.

    How: tap a card in your Hand, then the Face-Up Card you want to swap it with (or drag one onto the other). Your Face-Down Cards can never be swapped or looked at. Tap READY when you're done; play starts once everyone is ready.

    Why it matters: you play your Hand first, while the Deck keeps topping it up, but your Face-Up Cards only come into play near the end, when you have no Hand left to fall back on. That's when you need your strongest cards.

    Put face-up: your best cards, the power cards first (Joker, 3, 2, 10), then Aces and Kings. With them on the table you can beat almost anything when it counts, and a 10 or a 2 gets you out of a big Pile.

    Keep in your Hand: your low cards (4s, 5s, 6s, 7s). They're easy to get rid of early, while the Pile is still low and the Deck keeps refilling you.

    Pairs: two or three of the same rank face-up can be played together in one go, so matching cards on the table clear faster.

    Remember: everyone can see your Face-Up Cards, so opponents know what you'll have at the end, and you can read theirs too. If someone has stacked power cards face-up, try to leave them a big Pile before they reach them.

    2 (Reset) ⭐: Can be played on anything (except a Jack). Resets the Pile so anything can be played on it!
    3 (Transparent) ⭐: Can be played on anything (except a 6). Transparent — the next player must match or beat whatever card is underneath it instead.
    4 (Low): The most restrictive card. Can be played on a 2, 3, 4, 6, or 7 — and nothing else.
    5 (Drop Base): The next player must beat whatever card is at the very bottom (base) of the Pile. If that base card is an 8, the 5s played act as 8s instead — the number of 5s determines how many players get skipped. If the base card is a 9, the 5s act as 9s instead — an odd number reverses direction, an even number maintains it.
    6 (Evens): The next player must play an even card (2, 4, 6, 8, 10, Queen, Ace, or Joker).
    7 (7 or Lower): The next player must play a card of rank 7 or lower — a Joker can also always be played here, regardless of the constraint.
    8 (Skip): Playing multiple 8s together skips that many opponents. Skip everyone at the table and it becomes your turn again.
    9 (Reverse): An odd number of 9s reverses the direction, while an even number of 9s maintains direction.
    10 (Burner) ⭐: Instantly burns and clears the Pile permanently, and the player of the 10 then plays another card!
    Jack (Odds): The next player must play an odd card (3, 5, 7, 9, Jack, King, or Joker).
    Queen: Standard high card (Rank 12).
    King: Standard high card (Rank 13).
    Ace ⭐: The highest ranking standard card (Rank 14).
    4-of-a-Kind Burn: Four identical ranks played in a row burn the Pile immediately.
    Joker (Duel): Playable on anything! Forces a targeted opponent to pick up the Pile. If they counter with a Joker, it is deflected back! If the target has no Joker and picks up, the player who played the Joker gets another turn. All Jokers used in a duel are permanently burnt.

    1. The Swap Phase is Critical: Always put your strongest cards (2s, 3s, 10s, Aces, and Jokers) on your Face-Up Cards. They are your ultimate safety net for the Face-Up Table Phase.

    2. Cross-Phase Combos: When the Deck is depleted and all cards in your Hand match one or more Face-Up Cards, you can select and play them simultaneously in one massive turn!

    3. Snap Burn Vigilance: Watch the Pile closely. If you hold enough cards in your Hand to make four of a kind with the card(s) on top of the Pile, tap Snap Burn immediately, out of turn, to burn the Pile!

    4. Joker Timing: Don't waste your Joker early on a small Pile. Wait until an opponent is down to their last Hand or table cards with a large Pile accumulated.

    5. Bonus Draw: If you draw another card matching the rank you just played, you'll be offered a free follow-up play with it before the turn passes on — a nice way to chain extra plays in a single turn.

    Lives: win a game and you move on to the next round. Lose one and you lose a life and face the same bot again. Run out of lives and the run is over. The hearts and round number sit in the top-left corner of the table.

    One Gauntlet a day: the first mode you start on a day is your Gauntlet for that day (UK time). The other modes open again at midnight. You can retry that mode after running out of lives, until you beat it.

    Finish what you start: while a run is in progress you can't start a different mode. A run ends when you beat every bot or run out of lives. At midnight any run in progress ends and the next day starts fresh.

    Leaving: a game you start and don't finish counts as a loss, so leaving a game you're losing still costs a life. Closing the app is fine: the Gauntlet remembers exactly where you were, mid-game or between games.

    Rewards: each mode's first clear gives its Diamonds, avatar and card frame, which only its champions can have. After that, every day you beat it pays its daily Diamonds. Every bot you beat also gives XP: 40 Easy, 80 Medium, 120 Hard, 200 Boss.

    Offline: you can play the Easy Gauntlet with no signal or signed out, just without Diamonds or rewards. Hard and Boss need you to be signed in.

    Good to know: Gauntlet wins don't count toward unlocking bot difficulties or normal Vs Bots Diamonds. Every bot you beat, in any mode, counts on the Gauntlet leaderboard, even in runs you don't finish.

    Challenges: Gauntlet Champion, Hard Gauntlet Champion and Boss Gauntlet Champion (first clears) are in the Bots tab; the daily Gauntlet win is in the Daily tab.

    Ranked is one-on-one online against a real player, for your rating. Sign in, open Ranked on the home screen and press Find Game: you’re matched with the next player looking.

    Rating: everyone starts at 500. Win and it goes up, lose and it goes down. Beating a higher-rated player earns more than beating a lower one, and losing to a lower-rated player costs more. A draw moves both ratings toward each other, with no bonus.

    Win bonus: every win adds +10 on top.

    Win streak bonus: wins in a row add a one-off extra: +5 at 2 wins, +10 at 3, +15 at 5, and +20 at 10 and every 10 after. A losing streak costs nothing extra.

    Tiers: Novice, Bronze (500+), Silver (1000+), Gold (1500+), Platinum (1800+) and Master (2000+). Reaching Silver, Gold, Platinum and Master each earns that crown avatar.

    Rewards: a win pays 💎 20 and XP (+25 for any Ranked game, +150 for a win). Ranked games also count toward the Ranked challenges and the Ranked leaderboard.

    Leaving: leaving a Ranked game counts as a loss.

    If your app closes: a Medium bot keeps your seat. Come back before it has taken 5 turns and you carry on; after that you’re out of that game and finish last.

    Fair play: the server deals the cards and checks every move. A game that fails the checks isn’t scored, and the same two players are scored at most 5 times a day.

    Play Friends is your own table with the people you choose. Open Play Friends on the home screen.

    Hosting: press Host New Room and share the 6-digit code or the invite link. The host adds bots, sets the turn timer and starts the game.

    Joining: type a friend’s code, tap their invite link, or accept an invite in your Inbox. You don’t need an account to join a friend’s room.

    Players: up to 4 at a table. Empty seats can be filled with bots.

    Friends list: Menu → Friends shows who’s online. Invite a friend straight into your room, or tap their “In a match” status to watch their game.

    If someone’s app closes: a Medium bot keeps their seat until they’re back. After 5 bot turns they’re out of that game.

    Rewards: finishing and winning earn XP, and the games count toward your challenges.

    A Best Of Series is a run of Play Friends games between two players, played for Diamonds.

    Who can play: exactly two signed-in players, both Lvl 20 or above, with no bots at the table.

    Starting one: in the room, the host picks Best of 3, 5 or 7; the other player accepts or declines.

    Level requirements: Best of 3: Level 20 · Best of 5: Level 35 · Best of 7: Level 45. Both players must qualify.

    Entry and prize: each player pays 💎 30 (Best of 3), 💎 50 (Best of 5) or 💎 70 (Best of 7). The game matches the pot, so the series winner takes 💎 120, 💎 200 or 💎 280.

    Playing: first to 2 wins (Best of 3), 3 wins (Best of 5) or 4 wins (Best of 7). Both players press Ready before the next game starts, and the score sits in the top-left corner. A draw doesn’t count for either player. If a series runs very long, the player ahead wins it; if it’s level, both get their entry back.

    Calling it off: before the first game, either player can call it off and both entries go back.

    Leaving: after the first game, leaving hands the series to the other player.

    If your app closes: a Medium bot plays your seat, using the full turn time for each move. Come back within 5 of its turns and you carry on; after that the series goes to the other player.

    Limit: you can start up to 5 series a day.

    House Rules is a casual Play Friends mode where the host assigns powers to every rank, chooses the Joker behaviour and decides whether four of a kind Burns.

    Everyone sees the completed rules and must press Ready before the match starts. Any host change resets Ready. Signed-in hosts can save up to 3 named variants; ShitHead Deluxe is the only built-in preset.

    You can play with friends, with a mixture of friends and bots, or against up to 3 bots. Bots dynamically value the selected powers according to their difficulty.

    Important: House Rules is unranked, gives no Challenge progress and awards no Diamonds.

    Base Card: The card at the very bottom of the Pile — the first one played since the last Burn. Normally irrelevant, but a 5 drops the target down to it, so the next player must beat the Base Card instead of the top one.

    Best Of Series: Play Friends only: two players play Best of 3 at Lvl 20, Best of 5 at Lvl 35, or Best of 7 at Lvl 45. Both players must meet the level requirement. Each pays an entry, the game matches the pot, and the series winner takes it all.

    Blind Card: See Face-Down Cards.

    Bonus Draw: If the card you draw right after playing matches the rank you just played, you get offered a free follow-up play with it before the turn passes — a way to chain extra plays in one turn.

    Boss Gauntlet: A daily run against 3 Boss bots with 1 life, unlocked at Level 50 after beating Easy once and Hard three times. Beat all three to complete the run. Choose it on the Gauntlet page; you may enter one Gauntlet mode per day.

    Burn: The entire Pile is removed from the game permanently — not picked up by anyone. Caused by a 10, by four of a kind, or by a Joker duel. Whoever burnt it plays again immediately on an empty Pile — unless that was their last card, in which case they finish and the next player carries on.

    Card History: The circle in the left column on the table. It shows the card you have to beat; tap it to open the list of recent plays.

    Card Powers: The ⓘ button on the table: what every card does. Press and hold any card on the table for the same information.

    Challenges: Goals that pay Diamonds: Getting Started, daily and weekly picks, Bots, Ranked and Friends milestones. Menu → Challenges shows every one and what it asks for.

    Constraint: A restriction the previous card placed on your turn. A 6 forces an EVEN card, a Jack forces an ODD card, and a 7 forces a card of rank 7 or lower. The banner shows the active constraint.

    Custom: Menu → Custom: equip the avatars, tables, card backs, frames, decks, effects and emotes you own. Press and hold any item there (or in the Shop) for a big preview.

    Daily Challenges: Three new goals every UK day (Challenges → Daily tab), each paying Diamonds and XP.

    Deck: The face-down stack you draw from. After each turn you top your Hand back up to three cards while the Deck lasts. Once it's empty, no more topping up — and your Table Cards become reachable.

    Diamonds: The game’s currency (💎). Earned from wins, challenges, the daily login streak and the Gauntlet; spent in the Shop on cosmetics or gifts for friends.

    Direction: Which way turn order flows around the table. A 9 flips it — an odd number played at once reverses it, an even number leaves it unchanged. A 5 played on a 9 base, or a 3 played over a 9, both act as a 9 for this purpose too.

    Draw: When the same cards keep going round and nobody can ever get out, the game ends in a draw: nobody wins and nobody is the ShitHead.

    Duel (Joker Duel): When you play a Joker you choose an opponent to pick up the Pile. If that opponent holds a Joker they may counter with it, and the Pile bounces back to you instead. Both Jokers are burnt either way. Whoever wins the duel plays next; if their Joker was their last card, they finish and the next player carries on.

    Face-Down Cards: Three cards dealt beneath your Face-Up Cards that nobody ever sees. Only playable once your Hand, the Deck and your Face-Up Cards are all gone. You flip one blind — if it isn't legal, you pick up the Pile.

    Face-Up Cards: Three cards on the table in front of you, visible to everyone. Only playable once your Hand and the Deck are both empty. Because you play them last, the Swap Phase is your chance to stock them with strong cards.

    Four Of A Kind: All four cards of one rank on top of the Pile burn it, whether they were played together or across several turns. See Burn and Snap Burn.

    Gauntlet: Vs Bots games in a row against tough bots, with a few lives: Easy (5 bots, 3 lives), Hard (5 bots, 2 lives, Lvl 30) or Boss (3 Boss bots, 1 life, Lvl 50). One a day. See The Gauntlet above.

    Hand: The cards only you can see, held off the table. You play from here first, and top back up to three from the Deck after each turn while the Deck lasts.

    Hard Gauntlet: A daily run against 5 tough bots with 2 lives, unlocked at Level 30 after beating Easy once. Losing costs a life and you retry that bot. Choose it on the Gauntlet page; you may enter one Gauntlet mode per day.

    House Rules: A casual Play Friends mode where the host customises card powers. It is unranked, gives no Challenge progress and awards no Diamonds. Bots follow the same custom rules.

    Joker: The strongest card. Play it on anything, then choose an opponent to pick up the Pile. See Duel.

    Joker Effect: A cosmetic animation that plays over the table whenever you play a Joker, bought in the Shop’s Joker tab. Everyone at the table sees it, online too.

    Last Card: A player down to one card in total gets a pulsing ☝️ LAST CARD badge and a warning, so nobody sneaks out unnoticed.

    Leaderboard: Menu → Leaderboard: three tabs, Ranked (rating), Challenges (challenges completed) and Gauntlet (bots beaten). Reach the top 10, #3, #2 or #1 and a congratulations message lands in your Inbox.

    Level: You earn XP for finishing games, winning, Ranked, the Gauntlet and challenges. Levels run from 1 to 99; every level up pays Diamonds, and some levels unlock free items (see Level Rewards).

    Level Rewards: Free things you unlock by levelling up: 2x game speed at Lvl 5 and 4x at Lvl 10 (signed in), a second saved look at Lvl 10 and a third at Lvl 20, and earn-only avatars (Rookie Rogue 10, Card Shark 25, Burn King 50, Chaos Jester 75, The ShitHead 99) and card backs (First Burn 10, Shark’s Mark 30, Inferno 50, Chaos Crown 70, Master of the Pile 99). Your level badge also changes colour at 20, 40, 60, 80 and 99.

    Lives: Your hearts in the Gauntlet. You start with 3; losing a game costs one and you replay the same bot.

    No Power: In House Rules, the rank behaves as a normal card with no special effect. Unlike Please Select, it is a valid completed choice.

    Pick Up: Taking the entire Pile into your Hand because you had no legal move. The setback of the game. If your Hand was already empty, you must also take one Face-Up Card (or several of the same rank) along with it.

    Pile: The face-up stack in the middle that everyone plays onto. Your card must match or beat the top card of the Pile — if you can't, you take the whole Pile into your Hand.

    Play Friends: Online rooms with friends: share the room code or an invite link, add bots to fill empty seats, and play a Best Of Series once you're both Lvl 20.

    Play Matrix: The ▦ button on the table: a grid showing which card can go on which.

    Please Select: An unfinished House Rules setting. A match cannot start while any rank, Joker or four-of-a-kind rule still says Please Select.

    Power Cards: The five strongest cards — 2 (Reset), 3 (Transparent), 10 (Burner), Ace (highest rank) and Joker. Worth saving rather than spending, and worth putting in your Face-Up Cards during the Swap Phase.

    Quick Start: The one-minute tutorial for new players. The Tutorial page has longer lessons on every rule.

    Rank: A card's value, ignoring suit. Suits never matter in ShitHead — only rank. That's also why several cards of the same rank can be played together in one turn.

    Ranked: One-on-one online games against a real player, scored with a rating. Win and your rating goes up; lose and it goes down. Leaving a Ranked game counts as a loss.

    Rating: Your Ranked score (everyone starts at 500). It decides your Tier and your place on the Ranked leaderboard.

    Recruit: Share your invite link (Profile or Friends). When a new player joins with it and plays 3 games, you both get Diamonds. Recruit 5 for the Recruiter avatar.

    Reset: The 2. It can be played on almost anything (except a Jack) and clears the target, so the next player may follow it with any card at all.

    Seasonal Event: Halloween, Christmas, Diwali and more: while an event is on, its themed items are sold in the Shop’s Seasonal tab, and winning 3 games earns that event’s avatar.

    Select All Of A Rank: With it on (the round button left of your hand count, or S on a keyboard), tapping a card selects every card of that rank in your Hand.

    ShitHead: The loser — the last player still holding cards once everyone else has emptied their Hand, Face-Up and Face-Down cards.

    Skip: The 8. The next player misses their turn.

    Snap Burn: Completing four of a kind out of turn. If the top of the Pile already shows some of a rank and you hold the rest, a Snap Burn button appears and you can burn the Pile immediately, even when it isn't your turn.

    Stand-In: If a player's app closes during an online game, a Medium bot plays their seat until they're back. After 5 of its turns the absent player is out of that game.

    Swap Phase: The setup round before play begins, where you trade cards between your Hand and your Face-Up Cards. Since Face-Up Cards are played last, this is where you protect your Power Cards. Press READY when done.

    Table Cards: Collective term for your Face-Up and Face-Down Cards — everything in front of you rather than in your Hand.

    Tier: Your Ranked rank from your rating: Novice, Bronze (500+), Silver (1000+), Gold (1500+), Platinum (1800+) and Master (2000+).

    Transparent Card: The 3. It sets no target of its own, so the next player must beat whatever card sits underneath it. Playable on anything except a 6.

    Vs Bots: Games against computer players at Easy, Medium, Hard or Boss. Win games to unlock the next difficulty. Works offline too.

    Weekly Challenges: Five goals every UK week (Monday to Sunday) on the Challenges page, each paying Diamonds and XP.

    Win Streak: Wins in a row. In Ranked a streak adds bonus rating on top of the win: +5 at 2 wins, +10 at 3, +15 at 5 and +20 at 10 and every 10 after.
